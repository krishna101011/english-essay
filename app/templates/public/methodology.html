Feedback methodology

Two feedback layers

Local checker: spelling, punctuation, and basic grammar are checked by a local grammar-checking engine, immediately and without sending your text to any AI provider.

AI feedback: if you've connected an AI provider, it reviews sentence structure and vocabulary — deliberately not re-flagging what the local checker already caught — and produces the four rubric scores below. On a revision, it's only asked to review the paragraphs that changed since your last submission, to keep feedback focused and fast.

Rubric dimensions (0–100 each)

  • Grammar: correctness of sentence-level grammar beyond simple spelling/punctuation slips.
  • Vocabulary: word choice — variety, precision, and appropriateness for the register of the piece.
  • Structure: paragraph organization, transitions, and how well the essay is put together as a whole.
  • Clarity: how easy the writing is to follow and understand on a first read.

Roughly: 85–100 is a strong foundation, 70–84 is developing well, 50–69 marks a useful next step, and below 50 is a small starting point to build from. Each score comes with a "next best action" pointing at the single lowest-scoring dimension.

AI scores are guidance, not a grade

These scores are produced by a general-purpose AI model and are meant to guide revision — they are not an objective or authoritative grade, they can vary between submissions of similar quality, and they should not be treated as equivalent to a teacher's assessment.

Which AI provider is used?

You bring your own API key from a supported provider. See Privacy and AI data use for exactly what gets sent to it and how your key is protected.
